Agentic AI

Adviser or decision-maker Commentary

A fork in the road, not a speed setting.

The question is not how fast agents arrive but which kind the industry chooses to build. An agent that supplies a human with timely, reliable information is a different technology — economically and institutionally — from one that acts on its own authority.

Project Syndicate · 28 March 2025

Two Models for Agentic AI

Daron Acemoglu

Acemoglu argues that agentic AI presents a crossroads: as an adviser to human decision-makers it could deliver real gains, while fully autonomous agents bring foreseeable problems that would erode much of the benefit the technology might otherwise offer.

Read the commentary→

Model one

AI as adviser

The agent gathers, filters and presents. A person still decides, and still carries the consequences.

  • Human judgment stays in the loop, so accountability has somewhere to sit
  • Gains show up as better-informed decisions rather than removed headcount
  • Errors surface as bad advice a person can reject

Model two

AI as autonomous decision-maker

The agent transacts, commits and acts. The human is downstream of the decision, not upstream of it.

  • Accountability becomes diffuse when no person authorised the action
  • Errors compound before anyone is positioned to catch them
  • The displacement case strengthens without the productivity case improving
